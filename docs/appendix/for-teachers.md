# For Teachers

Information for teachers who are interested in incorporating this workshop (or something similar) into their own lesson plans.

## Find suitable documents to describe

We designed this workshop to be taught in-person, which allowed us to present the students with physical objects to describe. 
This gives the exercise a tactile component in a sort-of real-world setting, which is usually appreciated by the students. 
This means that to teach the workshop, you will have to find some interesting objects for your students to describe.

When you are deciding on which documents to use in the workshop, you'll obviously have to work with what's available to you.
Exactly how many documents you will need for the exercise will depend on the size of your class, and how large you want to allow the groups to become.
We believe it helps if you can try to find a set of documents that you are interested in, and that you think your students may also connect to im some way.

The most important thing, however, is that all of the documents in the set also share at least one connection with another document in the set. 
In our case, some records share band members, others share song titles, etc.
Take note of these connections, and try to find good identitfyers for them (e.g., on [WikiData](https://www.wikidata.org)).
You will need to use these identifiers to replace ours when you are editing 'Step 1.' in the instructions (see also below).
